# Korean Academic Style Guardrails

Use these rules when revising Korean or Korean-English bilingual thesis prose.

## Prefer

- concrete field objects before abstract claims
- active author judgment
- short and medium sentences mixed naturally
- data used as evidence, not decoration
- Korean academic terms tied to real systems or methods
- clear transitions between literature, data, and interpretation

## Avoid

- generic balanced prose with no thesis-specific object
- repeated endings and symmetrical sentence rhythm
- paragraphs that only list authors
- overuse of passive or impersonal wording
- broad definitions that do not help the user's research question
- casual paraphrase that weakens academic tone
- adding citations only to make a sentence look scholarly

## Paragraph Rhythm

Good Korean thesis paragraphs often contain:

1. one concrete research scene or concept
2. one citation, data point, or observed problem
3. one interpretation by the author
4. one narrow link to the next variable, section, or chapter

Do not force every paragraph into the same shape. Vary sentence length and paragraph structure according to the section's job.

## Citation Tone

Do not overstuff citations. A cited sentence should explain why the source matters for this thesis.

Weak pattern:

- "A, B, and C all discussed this topic."

Better pattern:

- "A's finding matters here because it explains why the user's data should be interpreted as a service-design problem rather than only as an individual ability problem."

## Translation And Bilingual Drafts

When moving between Korean and English:

- preserve the claim before polishing style
- keep technical terms consistent
- do not translate institution-specific or database names unless the user asks
- mark uncertain terms for user review
- avoid importing English journal rhetoric into undergraduate Korean thesis prose
