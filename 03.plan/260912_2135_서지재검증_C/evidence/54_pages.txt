Abstract 
 
Eu Wang Kim 
Major in Smart Planning, Construction & Information System 
Department of Smart Cities  
The Graduate School, Chung-Ang University 
 
 
Reviewing construction contracts requires expertise in both 
engineering and law, yet many organizations lack specialized professionals. 
While Large Language Models (LLMs) have potential, they often struggle to 
integrate domain-specific knowledge or produce reliable outputs for complex 
contracts. Addressing these challenges, this study introduces the Clause-
Oriented Review with Agent-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation 
(CORA-RAG) system, which enhances analysis accuracy and reliability 
through three key components: (1) integrating structured domain knowledge 
via a knowledge-enhanced RAG pipeline aligned with Standard Contract 
Forms (SCFs); (2) employing classification-based filtering combined with 
hybrid dense-sparse retrieval mechanisms to improve retrieval accuracy; and 
(3) utilizing a multi-agent LLM framework with a multi-stage generation 
process that includes context refinement and agent collaboration. 
CORA-RAG's knowledge base comprises over 2,000 text segments 

=== PDF PAGE 6 ===
ii 
mapped to SCFs, enabling precise interpretation of contractual provisions. 
The system uses a multi-agent approach, with each agent LLM focusing on 
different aspects of analysis, such as semantic understanding, context 
analysis, and risk assessment. The structured analysis progresses through 
stages: detecting differences, refining context, addressing practical and legal 
aspects, and conducting detailed risk and opportunity assessments—all 
facilitated by agent collaboration in a comprehensive generation framework. 
Validation involved quantitative evaluation using Retrieval-
Augmented Generation Answering System (RAGAS) metrics and expert 
assessments. Using Oman's General Conditions as a case study, performance 
evaluations showed significant improvements: a 52.6% increase in Context 
Recall and a 48.3% increase in Faithfulness compared to basic RAG 
approaches, particularly in complex reasoning and multi-context 
understanding. Classification-based filtering combined with hybrid retrieval 
methods reduced irrelevant data early in the process, while the multi-agent 
LLM framework enhanced the reliability and accuracy of content generation. 
Expert evaluations from thirteen construction industry professionals, 
including contractors, clients, and consultants, indicated that the system 
operates at an analytical level equivalent to junior professionals with 2–4 
years of experience. Experts confirmed the system's utility in expediting 

=== PDF PAGE 7 ===
iii 
basic contract analysis and enhancing work efficiency by reducing review 
time. It effectively assists practitioners, especially those with less experience, 
in navigating complex contracts by identifying relevant clauses and 
highlighting deviations. However, limitations were noted in interpreting 
nuanced language and conducting sophisticated legal analyses and risk 
assessments—areas typically requiring more experienced professionals. 
Key contributions of this study include developing an SCF-based 
structured knowledge framework, enhancing retrieval accuracy through 
classification-based filtering combined with hybrid dense-sparse retrieval 
techniques, and implementing a context-aware, multi-stage answer 
generation process via a multi-agent LLM framework. By integrating 
structured knowledge with advanced agent collaboration in both retrieval and 
generation, the CORA-RAG system provides construction professionals with 
a reliable and efficient tool for detailed contract reviews, addressing the 
growing need for automated expertise in construction contract analysis. 
 
 
Keywords: AI-driven Review, Contract Management, Retrieval-
Augmented Generation (RAG), Large Language Models (LLM), 
Knowledge Base

1. Introduction 
1.1 Background and Motivation 
Construction projects often encounter disputes due to inadequate 
contract 
management, 
which 
includes 
ambiguous 
terms, 
poor 
communication, and misinterpretations of obligations. Errors and omissions 
in contract documents are primary causes of disputes in major construction 
markets, significantly impacting project success by escalating costs and 
delaying timelines (Arcadis, 2022). Recent industry reports have highlighted 
a dramatic rise in construction dispute costs over a short period (Arcadis, 
2023). 
To address these challenges, improving the efficiency of contract 
management is essential. The emergence of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and 
Large Language Models (LLMs) offers promising approaches to provide 
expert knowledge and increase productivity. Specifically, AI-driven contract 
analysis tools have the potential to streamline contract review processes and 
strengthen risk management capabilities. By automating repetitive tasks, 
these technologies enable construction professionals to manage contracts 
more efficiently. 
However, applying current LLMs in contract management presents 

=== PDF PAGE 17 ===
2 
limitations. As general-purpose models, they raise concerns about the 
trustworthiness and accuracy of their outcomes when applied to precision-
critical contractual contexts. The lack of domain-specific knowledge can lead 
to missing subtle meanings in complex clauses, hindering the models’ ability 
to interpret contracts holistically. This difficulty makes it challenging to 
provide reliable analysis across entire documents, as nuances and 
interdependencies between clauses may be overlooked or misinterpreted. 
 
1.2 Research Objectives 
This research aims to overcome the significant challenges in 
automating construction contract reviews by introducing a structured 
knowledge-integrated Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline 
tailored for construction contracts. The proposed system, named Clause-
Oriented Review with Agentic RAG (CORA-RAG), integrates domain 
expertise with the advanced capabilities of language models. The study 
focuses on three primary objectives: 
1. Develop a Structured Knowledge Framework: Systematically 
organize technical and legal expertise by mapping contract clauses to relevant 
analyses 
and 
practical 
considerations. 
This 
framework 
ensures 

=== PDF PAGE 18 ===
3 
comprehensive coverage of essential aspects in contract review and enables 
the system to interpret complex contractual relationships with clarity and 
precision. It utilizes Standard Contract Forms (SCFs) such as FIDIC and 
Oman’s General Conditions. 
2. Create a Robust Retrieval Mechanism: Maintain accuracy when 
dealing with complex and extensive contract data by employing a two-stage 
retrieval process. Initially, classification-based filtering narrows the search 
scope within the knowledge base, enhancing focus and reducing irrelevant 
data. Subsequently, a hybrid search approach combines dense retrieval 
(semantic matching) and sparse retrieval (keyword-based matching) to 
ensure both contextual understanding and precise identification of critical 
contractual elements. 
3. Establish Reliable Generation Processes: Integrate structured 
references into the system’s outputs to enhance response accuracy and 
reliability. By refining retrieved contexts and incorporating domain-specific 
knowledge, the system produces analysis outputs that align with legal, 
technical, and practical requirements, ensuring that generated insights are not 
only accurate but also actionable in professional settings. 
Through these objectives, the proposed CORA-RAG system aims to 

=== PDF PAGE 19 ===
4 
enhance the efficiency of contract reviews by addressing key challenges such 
as the lack of domain expertise, maintaining accuracy in analysis, and 
ensuring reliable results. The CORA-RAG system follows a step-by-step 
process: it begins by identifying differences between new contracts and 
standard forms, refines the context by analyzing interpretation, practical 
issues, and legal aspects, and finally performs a detailed review—including 
risk and opportunity assessment—to ensure a thorough understanding of the 
contract. By combining structured knowledge with advanced LLM 
technologies, CORA-RAG bridges the gap between traditional manual 
methods and modern automated tools, enhancing the practicality and 
reliability of construction contract reviews.

2. Research Background 
2.1 Challenges in Construction Contract Review  
The importance of construction contract review has grown 
significantly as the value of disputes escalates. This process requires expert 
knowledge that is both timely and appropriately scoped. Engineers frequently 
encounter challenges with legal implications, while lawyers may struggle to 
grasp the technical details (Seppälä, 2023, p. 162). The complexity of 
international projects is further compounded by the need to interpret 
contracts across various legal systems, necessitating a thorough 
understanding of diverse contractual forms and legal principles (Dmaidi et 
al., 2013). 
Expertise in construction contract review involves understanding, 
analyzing, and interpreting complex contract provisions that intertwine 
technical and legal aspects. Professionals must possess deep knowledge of 
construction processes, contract law, industry practices, and specific project 
contexts. This integration is essential to identify risks, provide clear 
interpretations, and align with project objectives and the parties' intentions. 
The complexity is notably higher in FIDIC contracts, where provisions across 
different clauses are highly interconnected. FIDIC contracts serve as 

=== PDF PAGE 23 ===
8 
fundamental building blocks in construction project delivery worldwide, 
leading to extensive studies and applications (Migliaccio & Shrestha, 2022, 
p. 283). 
Understanding these contracts demands a comprehensive approach 
to analyze how various clauses interact (Baker et al., 2013, p. 30). The 
presence of numerous cross-references and complex sentence structures 
increases the risk of misunderstanding (Rasslan & Nassar, 2017). Contract 
interpretation principles require reviewers to adopt a holistic approach rather 
than focusing on isolated words or phrases, necessitating a thorough 
comprehension of the entire document (McMeel, 2011, p. 80). 
Table 1 illustrates the progressive expansion of FIDIC's contract 
suite from a single contract form in 1977 to 18 distinct works contracts by 
2023. This expansion reflects both the growing complexity of the 
construction industry and the increasing challenges faced by contract review 
professionals. The suite's growth shows a strategic pattern of diversification, 
from the traditional Red Book to specialized forms like the Gold Book for 
design-build-operate projects (2008) and the Emerald Book for underground 
works (2019). Each new addition targets specific project delivery methods or 
specialized construction sectors, significantly expanding the knowledge 
requirements for professionals. 

=== PDF PAGE 24 ===
9 
This expansion has created two major challenges: professionals must 
now maintain expertise across a wider range of contract types, each with 
specific provisions, and understand the complex interrelationships between 
different contract forms. The periodic updates and reprints, including the 
2022 reprints of the 2017 Rainbow Suite, further necessitate continuous 
learning to maintain current expertise across this expanding contract portfolio.

2.2 AI and LLM Applications in Contract Management  
Automated review in construction contract management is evolving, 
presenting both technological advancements and implementation challenges. 
Early research prominently featured rule-based Natural Language Processing 
(NLP) approaches for risk detection, compliance checking, and clause 
analysis (Hamie & Abdul-Malak, 2018; Lee et al., 2019; Serag et al., 2010; 
Xu & Cai, 2018; Zhang et al., 2016). These methods were later extended to 
include uncertainty analysis and automated compliance checking (Faraji et 
al., 2021; Guo et al., 2021). 
Conventional machine learning methods began with litigation 
outcome prediction using various classifiers (Arditi & Pulket, 2005). 
Subsequent studies implemented Support Vector Machines (SVM), Naïve 
Bayes, and other machine learning approaches for compliance checking and 
requirements classification (Candaş & Tokdemir, 2022; Hassan et al., 2020; 
Salama & El-Gohary, 2016). Hybrid approaches combining rule-based and 
machine learning methods were also explored (Choi et al., 2021; Hassan & 
Le, 2020). 
Deep learning and transformer-based models have further enhanced 
contract analysis capabilities. Models like Bidirectional Long Short-Term 

=== PDF PAGE 26 ===
11 
Memory (Bi-LSTM), Conditional Random Fields (CRF), and Bidirectional 
Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) have been utilized to 
improve performance in tasks such as clause classification and information 
extraction (Moon et al., 2022; Xue et al., 2022; Zheng et al., 2022; Zhou et 
al., 2023). Recent studies have advanced the field using architectures like 
DeBERTa and pre-trained BERT models for risk prediction and dispute 
classification (Ko et al., 2024; Pham & Han, 2023; Qi et al., 2024; Zhong et 
al., 2024). 
Recently, LLMs with knowledge-augmented approaches have 
emerged in contract management (Wong et al., 2023; Zheng et al., 2023). 
While these advancements offer significant potential, current methods still 
face substantial challenges: 
 
Insufficient Domain Expertise: LLMs, being trained on 
general datasets, often lack the specialized technical and 
legal knowledge required for in-depth contract analysis. 
 
Performance Degradation with Lengthy Documents: 
LLMs' accuracy notably drops when processing lengthy 
documents, with reported accuracy decreasing to 55% for 
documents exceeding ten paragraphs (Wang et al., 2024). 

=== PDF PAGE 27 ===
12 
 
Reliability Concerns: The probabilistic nature of LLM 
outputs leads to issues like hallucinations and inconsistent 
context understanding, which are critical in precision-
sensitive contractual contexts. 
These limitations are particularly evident in RAG systems, which 
continue to struggle with managing large volumes of unstructured 
information and maintaining consistent interpretation of complex contract 
relationships. This underscores the need for an improved methodology that 
effectively integrates domain expertise while ensuring reliable contract 
analysis capabilities. 
 
2.3 Related Work and Research Gaps  
The evolution from basic NLP techniques to advanced LLMs in AI-
driven contract management highlights the increasing importance of 
integrating domain-specific knowledge with structured information to 
enhance analysis performance. Effective contract review requires extensive 
expertise in both technical and legal domains, making knowledge integration 
particularly challenging. This reflects the principle that "knowledge is 
power" in expert systems, where system performance is more heavily reliant 

=== PDF PAGE 28 ===
13 
on domain knowledge quality than on reasoning capabilities (Brock, 2018). 
A fundamental limitation of LLMs is their struggle with domain-
specific knowledge that is not widely represented in their training data 
(Mallen et al., 2022). Kandpal et al. (2022) indicate that simply increasing 
model size does not significantly improve the recall of specialized or rare 
information. In the context of construction contracts, where specialized 
technical and legal expertise is essential, these limitations become especially 
significant. 
Several critical challenges have been identified: 
 
Difficulty in Organizing Domain Expertise: Construction 
contracts require the integration of both technical and legal 
knowledge to analyze specific provisions. Current systems 
struggle to incorporate and maintain such diverse expertise 
in a structured format. 
 
Accuracy 
Degradation 
with 
Complex 
Documents: 
Managing lengthy and complex documents remains 
problematic. Existing basic RAG approaches, which handle 
unstructured data, face limitations in effective information 
retrieval and matching, leading to decreased accuracy. 

=== PDF PAGE 29 ===
14 
 
Reliability Issues in LLM-Generated Content: Ensuring 
result reliability is a critical concern. LLM-based systems 
are prone to hallucinations and may misinterpret complex 
contractual 
relationships, 
necessitating 
sophisticated 
verification 
mechanisms 
to 
ensure 
accuracy 
and 
consistency (Zheng et al., 2023). 
Addressing these challenges requires an approach that effectively 
integrates structured domain knowledge into the AI models. This research 
proposes the development of a structured knowledge-integrated RAG 
pipeline that enhances the RAG process for construction contract review. The 
proposed solution focuses on: 
 
Implementing a Structured Knowledge Framework: 
Systematically organizing technical and legal expertise to 
provide a comprehensive knowledge base for analysis. 
 
Developing a Precise Retrieval Mechanism: Employing a 
two-stage retrieval process that combines classification-
based filtering with dense-sparse retrieval methods to 
maintain accuracy when dealing with complex and 
extensive contract data. 

=== PDF PAGE 30 ===
15 
 
Establishing Reliable Answer Generation Processes: 
Integrating structured references into the system's outputs 
to improve response accuracy and reliability. 
By bridging the gap between the challenges in practical contract 
review and the limitations of existing AI methods, this approach aims to 
enhance the practical applicability of automated contract review tools in 
construction projects. The integration of structured domain knowledge is 
expected to improve the accuracy, consistency, and reliability of AI-driven 
contract analysis, making it a valuable tool for professionals in the industry.

3. Retrieve-Focused Framework and Validation 
3.1 System Requirements 
To effectively address the challenges in automating construction 
contract reviews, the proposed system must meet several key requirements. 
These requirements focus on integrating expert knowledge, incorporating 
precise retrieval mechanisms, generating accurate and traceable outputs, and 
maintaining connections to reference sources—crucial for processing 
complex construction contracts. 
First, the system needs to integrate technical and legal 
expertise through a systematically organized knowledge structure. LLMs 
often struggle with domain-specific knowledge that isn't widely represented 
in their training data, especially in specialized fields like construction 
contracts. Therefore, integrating structured knowledge into the RAG pipeline 
is essential for accurately analyzing specific clauses and handling specialized 
terms. This ensures comprehensive coverage and precise interpretation of 
contract clauses. 
Second, 
the 
system 
must 
incorporate precise 
retrieval 
mechanisms to effectively extract relevant clauses and information from 
extensive text documents. To overcome limitations in handling large volumes 

=== PDF PAGE 35 ===
20 
of unstructured information and maintaining consistent interpretations, the 
system should employ a two-stage retrieval process. This includes 
classification-based filtering to narrow down pertinent sections and dense-
sparse retrieval methods to extract precise information, even from lengthy 
and complex documents with interconnected clauses. 
Third, the system must generate answers that are not only accurate 
but also traceable to their sources. Given the uncertainty inherent in LLM 
outputs, implementing reference-based answer generation ensures that all 
responses are supported by and linked to specific sections of the knowledge 
base. This approach enhances the reliability and trustworthiness of the 
automated analysis, allowing users to verify outputs and maintain confidence 
in the system's recommendations.

3.4 Retrieval Component  
This section covers the second requirement, inclusion of precise 
retrieval mechanisms. Combining classification-based filtering with hybrid 
search methods, this component accurately and efficiently retrieves relevant 
information from complex contract documents. 
The retrieval mechanism operates as a two-stage process. First, 
clause classification based on SCF classification narrows the search scope 
within the Knowledge Base, ensuring that only pertinent clauses are 
considered, reducing noise and improving efficiency. Second, a hybrid search 
combines dense vector embeddings and sparse keyword-based methods to 

=== PDF PAGE 47 ===
32 
precisely identify relevant information. Dense retrieval implements FAISS 
with the text-embedding-3-small model for semantic similarity searches, 
mapping textual data into high-dimensional vector space to capture 
contextual meaning. Sparse retrieval employs BM25—a well-established 
TF-IDF-based function that ranks documents according to query terms. 
This two-stage retrieval mechanism effectively targets specific 
domain knowledge, enhancing the accuracy and reliability of contract 
analysis. By processing input contracts through this combined classification 
and hybrid retrieval method—integrating both dense and sparse retrieval 
techniques with relationship-aware ranking that considers interconnections 
among contract elements—the system effectively identifies relevant clauses 
and associated expert knowledge. 
Furthermore, using SCF classification in the initial filtering reduces 
the search space and aligns the retrieval process with standardized contract 
structures. This alignment is crucial for maintaining consistency and ensuring 
that the retrieved information is contextually relevant. By structuring the 
retrieval mechanism in this way, the system efficiently handles the 
complexity and length of construction contracts, providing accurate and 
reliable analytical results.

3.5 Generation Component 
This section addresses the third requirement: generating accurate and 
traceable answers by maintaining connections to reference sources. The 
Generation Component is responsible for producing contract analyses based 
on the information prepared by the Retrieval Component and the structured 
knowledge base. 
After the Retrieval Component identifies relevant clauses and 
associated expert knowledge, the Generation Component performs a 
differential analysis between the input contract clauses and the corresponding 
clauses from the SCFs. By comparing the input contract to the SCFs, it 
identifies discrepancies such as modifications, additions, or omissions. 
Utilizing contextual information from the knowledge base—including expert 
analyses, legal information, and practical considerations—the Generation 
Component assesses the implications of these discrepancies. 
To ensure transparency and trustworthiness, each conclusion and 
recommendation is explicitly linked to specific sections of the knowledge 
base and SCF clauses. This explicit linkage allows users to easily trace the 
generated analyses back to their underlying sources, enhancing the reliability 
and credibility of the system's outputs.

3.7 Interim Discussion 
3.7.1 Performance Analysis 
By fulfilling the three requirements outlined in Section 3.1, the 
Structured Knowledge-Integrated RAG Pipeline effectively addresses 
significant challenges in automated contract analysis for construction 
contracts. Firstly, the systematic integration of expert knowledge through a 
well-organized knowledge structure enables the system to handle specialized 
technical and legal tasks with greater accuracy. By incorporating domain-
specific expertise into a knowledge base, the system mitigates the limitations 
of LLMs in dealing with specialized knowledge not widely represented in 
their training data, ensuring precise interpretation of contract clauses. 
Secondly, the incorporation of precise retrieval mechanisms 
addresses challenges in handling large volumes of unstructured information 
and maintaining consistent interpretations. By combining classification-
based filtering with hybrid dense-sparse retrieval techniques, the system 
mitigates limitations of basic RAG approaches, improving the retrieval of 
relevant clauses and contextual information. 
Thirdly, maintaining connections to reference sources enhances 
traceability, mitigating the probabilistic nature of LLM outputs and ensuring 

=== PDF PAGE 62 ===

the reliability. Through reference-based generation, the system allows users 
to trace conclusions back to original sources, strengthening trustworthiness 
where accuracy is paramount. 
 
3.7.2 Enhancements via Structured Information Integration 
The proposed method achieved Context Recall of 0.969 and 
Faithfulness of 0.893, compared to Basic RAG's 0.602. This demonstrates 
improved reference maintenance and reduced hallucinations. 
Figure 5 illustrates the comparative analysis of responses to "What

different models. 
The Base LLM offered only general environmental protection 
practices without addressing specific contractual requirements. It missed 
critical obligations like taking "all necessary measures," limiting pollution 
and noise damage, controlling emissions, and complying with the 
environmental impact statement. 
Both the Search-Augmented LLM and Basic RAG included key 
contractual obligations but diluted their responses with irrelevant information 
about local regulations, monitoring systems, and legal consequences. This

Search-Augmented LLM demonstrated even more pronounced variability, 
with a Context Precision standard deviation of ±0.297 and Context Recall 
standard deviation of ±0.392. This high variability undermines the reliability 
essential for tasks that require consistent precision, such as contract analysis. 
The proposed method often generated responses closely matching 
the contractual content. It specified that the Contractor must take all 
necessary measures to protect the environment both on and off site, limit 
damage from pollution and noise, ensure emissions don't exceed specified 
values, and comply with environmental impact statements. This

3.7.3 Limitations 
Despite the strong performance, certain limitations highlight areas 
for improvement. First, although the system achieves a high Context Recall 
(0.969), the relatively lower Context Precision of 0.625 indicates that the 
retrieval mechanism occasionally includes less relevant information. 
Additionally, the Answer Correctness metric of 0.750 suggests potential for 
improvement in generating accurate responses.

quantitative insights into the system's retrieval and answer generation

experts, highlighting the necessity for manual expert evaluations to ensure 
comprehensive assessment of analysis quality. 
Third, the system's effectiveness depends on the comprehensiveness 
of its knowledge base for SCFs, which may limit its applicability to contracts 
outside of this scope. This reliance on a specific knowledge base means the

limitations of current LLMs. These models often struggle with logical 
reasoning and understanding cause-and-effect relationships within contract 
terms. They may identify changes in specific clauses but fail to comprehend 
how these modifications impact interconnected provisions. 
To overcome these limitations, a multi-agent LLM framework is 
introduced. This approach employs multiple LLM agents assigned to specific 
tasks or domains—such as differential analysis, or risk assessment—
enhancing focus and expertise without altering the underlying models. 
Instead of fine-tuning, strategic prompt engineering and task-specific 
configurations are utilized to tailor each agent's function. 
By assigning specific roles to LLM agents and enabling 
collaboration, the system can better handle complex contractual contexts. 
Agents share information and build upon each other's outputs, distributing 
the workload and improving reasoning. Key aspects of this approach include:

4.4 Summary 
This chapter presented the CORA-RAG system, a multi-agent Large 
Language Model framework developed to automate and enhance the contract 
review process in the construction industry. By employing agents—such as 
those for textual difference analysis, semantic analysis, context evaluation, 
and risk assessment—the system facilitates efficient and comprehensive 
contract analysis. 
The pipeline architecture integrates these agents to ensure coherent 
progression through analysis stages, enhancing the accuracy and reliability 
of the outputs. Validation mechanisms involving domain experts are 
incorporated to maintain quality. 
The system was evaluated both through automated metrics and an 
expert assessment involving thirteen professionals from contractors, clients, 
and consulting firms. Results indicated that the system effectively improves 
efficiency and productivity, significantly reducing contract review time and 
assisting practitioners, particularly less experienced ones, in understanding 
complex contracts. 
However, the evaluation revealed that the system's analytical 
capabilities currently align with the proficiency level of junior professionals

5. Discussion  
5.1 Technical Performance Analysis 
5.1.1 System Architecture and Integration 
The CORA-RAG system tackles challenges in automating 
construction contract reviews by integrating refinement and generation 
components within a multi-agent LLM framework. agent LLMs focus on 
specific tasks like legal analysis, technical evaluation, and risk assessment, 
applying appropriate expertise to enhance accuracy and relevance. This 
specialization allows the system to handle complex contract clauses 
effectively. 
Experimental results show that the CORA-RAG system improves 
upon base LLMs and standard RAG approaches in automated metrics such 
as refinement quality and response generation. However, expert evaluations 
reveal that the system's real-world effectiveness aligns with the performance 
level of junior professionals with 2–4 years of experience. While the system 
enhances certain technical aspects, further development is needed to achieve 
higher proficiency levels in complex contract analysis. 
 

=== PDF PAGE 114 ===
99 
5.1.2 Multi-stage Generation Process 
The generation component employs a multi-stage process where 
agent LLMs refine contextual data and integrate expert knowledge from 
various domains. Agents handle tasks like differential analysis, and risk-
opportunity assessment, accurately identifying differences in contract clauses 
and ensuring coherence across technical and legal domains. 
Currently, the system provides insights encompassing technical and 
practical aspects but lacks depth in legal analysis and nuanced interpretations. 
Its performance is comparable to practitioners with 2–4 years of experience, 
indicating the need for further refinement to generate comprehensive legal 
insights. 
 
5.2 Practical Implications and Limitations 
5.2.1 System Benefits and Applications 
The CORA-RAG system automates the initial stages of contract 
review, accelerating the identification of relevant clauses by quickly 
processing large volumes of text and highlighting deviations. This reduces 
manual effort and time, enhancing the overall efficiency of contract 
management processes—particularly valuable in the construction industry, 

=== PDF PAGE 115 ===

where contractual documents are extensive and complex. 
By efficiently refining information and identifying clause changes, 
the system assists practitioners, especially those with less experience, in 
navigating complex contracts. It enhances transparency by documenting each 
agent's steps—such as sources consulted and reasoning applied—building 
trust in the automated review process. 
 
5.2.2 Current Limitations and Challenges 
Despite its strengths, the CORA-RAG system currently operates at 
an analytical level equivalent to junior professionals with 2–4 years of 
experience. It exhibits limitations in handling highly complex clauses, 
nuanced legal scenarios, and distinguishing between risks and opportunities, 
affecting its reliability in supporting decision-making processes that depend 
on accurate risk assessments and in-depth legal analyses. 
Improvements are needed in processing speed and user interface to 
facilitate seamless integration into professional workflows. Enhancing agent 
LLM performance is essential to elevate the system's analytical capabilities 
to match those of more experienced practitioners. 
While automated metrics show positive results, discrepancies with

measures for assessing real-world effectiveness. Manual expert evaluation 
remains crucial for ensuring high-quality contract analysis and guiding

to practitioners with 4–7 years of experience or more. This includes 
improving the ability to interpret nuanced contractual language, providing

6. Conclusion  
6.1 Research Summary and Achievements 
This research addressed three major challenges in automating 
construction contract review by developing a Structured Knowledge-
Integrated RAG Pipeline that integrates domain expertise with LLMs through 
a multi-agent architecture. 
Firstly, to tackle the challenge of organizing domain expertise, a 
structured knowledge framework was developed. This framework 
systematically integrates expert analyses, legal information, and practical 
considerations into a knowledge base aligned with SCFs. By mapping 
domain knowledge to specific SCF clauses and employing classification-
based filtering, the system effectively reduces irrelevant data early in the 
retrieval process, enhancing accuracy and efficiency compared to methods 
relying solely on dense and sparse searches without prior filtering. 
Secondly, to overcome the challenge of maintaining accuracy with 
complex and extensive contract documents, a two-stage refinement process 
within a multi-agent LLM system was implemented. Agents are assigned to 
tasks such as semantic analysis and context analysis, allowing the system to 
handle intricate contractual conditions. Enhancing context during refinement 

=== PDF PAGE 118 ===
103 
and integrating hybrid search techniques significantly improved information 
accuracy. Experimental validation showed that Context Recall reached 0.969 
and Faithfulness scored 0.893, indicating substantial improvements over 
basic RAG approaches and demonstrating the system's high accuracy with 
complex contracts. 
Thirdly, to address the challenge of ensuring reliability in LLM-
generated content, reliable generation processes were established through a 
multi-agent LLM framework. The system employs a multi-stage refinement 
pipeline where agent LLMs collaborate to align content generation with 
domain expertise. The reference-based generation process maintains explicit 
connections to source materials by citing specific clauses and referencing the 
knowledge base during content generation. This approach enhances 
traceability and trustworthiness, ensuring that all generated analyses can be 
directly linked back to authoritative sources. The system achieved an average 
score of 0.828 ± 0.087, indicating consistent outputs, though Context 
Precision of 0.625 and Answer Correctness of 0.750 suggest opportunities 
for refining retrieval precision and response generation. 
 
6.2 Research Contributions 

=== PDF PAGE 119 ===
104 
This research advances the field of construction contract 
management through three significant contributions: 
1. Structured Integration of Domain Knowledge: A framework was 
introduced for integrating domain expertise into automated contract review, 
transforming the organization and application of construction contract 
knowledge. By systematically organizing expert analyses, legal information, 
and practical considerations into a structured knowledge base using 
ontologies and metadata tagging, the system can access and apply expert 
interpretations, legal precedents, and practical guidelines during analysis. 
This enables comprehensive and reliable assessments by drawing on a rich 
repository of domain-specific knowledge. 
2. Enhanced Retrieval Accuracy through Classification-Based 
Filtering: Remarkable improvements in retrieval accuracy were achieved by 
implementing classification-based filtering combined with hybrid dense-
sparse retrieval techniques. By categorizing and filtering the knowledge base 
according to relevant SCF clauses, the system reduces irrelevant data early 
in the process, improving retrieval efficiency and accuracy. Notable 
performance gains were observed, with Context Recall improving by 52.6% 
and Faithfulness by 48.3%, setting new standards for automated document 
analysis, especially in the context of complex construction contracts. 

=== PDF PAGE 120 ===
105 
3. Reliable Generation Processes through Multi-Agent LLM 
Framework: A multi-agent LLM framework was developed to enhance the 
reliability and accuracy of content generation. By employing a multi-stage 
refinement pipeline with agent LLMs, the system aligns content generation 
with domain expertise. The reference-based generation process maintains 
explicit connections to source materials by citing specific clauses and 
referencing the knowledge base during content generation. This approach 
enhances traceability and trustworthiness, ensuring that all generated 
analyses can be directly linked back to authoritative sources. 
 
6.3 Research Limitations 
Despite its advancements, the CORA-RAG system has several 
limitations: 
1. Retrieval Precision and Answer Correctness: While Context 
Recall and Faithfulness scores are high, a Context Precision of 0.625 and 
Answer Correctness of 0.750 indicate that retrieval sometimes includes less 
relevant information, and responses may not always be fully accurate. 
Refining retrieval algorithms and improving answer generation processes are 
necessary to enhance precision and correctness. 

=== PDF PAGE 121 ===
106 
2. Dependence on Knowledge Base Scope: The system's 
effectiveness heavily relies on the comprehensiveness of its knowledge base 
concerning standard form contracts. This reliance may limit its applicability 
to contracts outside of this scope, such as customized or significantly 
modified contracts, reducing its practicality in varied contractual scenarios. 
3. Complexity of Multi-Agent LLM Framework: The multi-agent 
architecture, while enhancing reliability, introduces complexity in agent 
coordination and communication. Challenges may arise in ensuring seamless 
collaboration among agents, potentially affecting processing speed and 
efficiency. Further research is needed to optimize agent interactions and 
manage the computational overhead associated with the multi-agent system. 
 
6.4 Future Research 
To enhance the CORA-RAG system and address its limitations, 
future research should prioritize the following areas: 
1. Enhancing Agent LLM Capabilities: Improve the training and 
collaboration of agent LLMs to handle intricate clauses and subtle 
contractual nuances more effectively. Advanced training on diverse legal 
scenarios and optimization of agent communication protocols will enhance 

=== PDF PAGE 122 ===
107 
their interpretative abilities and processing efficiency. 
2. Improving Retrieval Precision and Response Generation: Develop 
more sophisticated retrieval algorithms and refine the generation processes 
to increase Context Precision and Answer Correctness. Optimizing the 
integration of the retrieval and generation components will improve 
information flow and relevance. Implementing advanced validation 
mechanisms can ensure the accuracy of generated content. 
3. Expanding the Knowledge Base: Extend the knowledge base 
beyond standard form contracts to include tailored and non-standard 
contracts. This expansion will enhance the system's practical applicability 
across a broader range of contractual scenarios, making it more versatile and 
valuable in the construction industry.

knowledge base, the CORA-RAG system can further bridge the gap between

건설계약 검토는 기술과 법률 분야의 전문지식을 요구하지만, 
현업에서는 전문인력 부족으로 많은 어려움을 겪고 있다. 대규모언어모
델(LLM)이 이러한 문제해결에 잠재력을 보이고 있으나, 복잡한 계약에서 
도메인지식을 효과적으로 통합하고 활용하여 신뢰할 수 있는 결과물을 
생성하는 데는 여전히 한계가 있다. 이러한 문제를 해결하기 위해 본 연
구에서는 CORA-RAG 시스템을 제안한다. 
CORA-RAG는 2,000개 이상의 텍스트 세그먼트를 포함하는 지식
베이스를 기반으로 계약조항의 해석이 가능하다. 해당 시스템은 멀티 에
이전트 접근 방식을 채택하여 의미 분석, 컨텍스트 분석, 위험 평가 등 
다양한 측면에서 분석을 수행한다. 분석은 차이점 식별, 컨텍스트 정제,
