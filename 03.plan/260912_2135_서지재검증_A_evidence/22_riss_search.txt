In the global construction industry, government policies have recently focused on smart construction technologies, such as those concerning the “smartization” of construction, improvements of productivity, and automation technologies. In addition, smart construction safety technologies (SCSTs) have been developed to ensure workers’ safety, under the initiative of the private sector. In regards to overseas occupational safety, wearable technologies have been developed for various types of industries, and the integrated platform developments needed to link them have become mainstream. In South Korea, individual companies are focusing on developing basic SCSTs and platforms for integrated control, aiming to prevent accidents in the construction field. The goal of this study was to identify the pros and cons of SCSTs through test bed operation and to derive improvement directions. Therefore, a test bed embedded with SCSTs was built and operated to provide effective safety management for small- and medium-sized sites exposed to fatal accidents. From analyzing the data from the test bed, it was found that it is difficult to change the tendencies of workers’ behaviors based solely on the introduction of SCSTs. This indicates that the effects of SCSTs are insignificant without the cooperation of workers. Based on the results derived through analysis of the pros and cons of SCSTs, the directions and guidelines were suggested for future use. This result can be used for future technology development directions, and policy establishment. Additionally, for the activation of SCSTs in the field, the cooperation of workers and the interest of managers remain essential factors, and improvements to the equipment are required. Department of Construction Law The Graduate School of Construction Legal Affairs KwangWoon University The Serious Accident Punishment Act was enacted to prevent accidents and protect the lives and health of citizens and workers by prescribing punishments for employers, managers, public officials, and corporations who violate safety and health measures while operating workplaces, public facilities, or public transportation, or handling hazardous raw materials or products, resulting in fatalities. However, the number of fatalities has not decreased since the law's implementation, raising questions about its effectiveness. Punishing managers alone fails to reduce worker casualties and is increasingly being used as a means of legal action to avoid prosecution after an accident, rather than preventing risks. Even this is limited to workplaces that can afford the legal fees of large law firms, while smaller businesses are left without legal protection and are thus left to fend for themselves. Furthermore, foreign workers, who face a high fatality rate due to language barriers, are excluded from the protections of the Serious Accident Punishment Act. Therefore, this study proposes the legalization of mobile safety inspections to establish a practical prevention system that aligns with the legislative objectives. Using smartphones, this system eliminates hazards in real time, conducts worksite inspections, records work logs, and stores, statistics, and analyzes on-site data to prevent industrial accidents. To improve safety awareness among on-site workers, it applies the Machinery and Equipment Act, the Special Act on the Safety and Maintenance of Facilities, and the Road Traffic Act to recommend on-site exclusion and safety training for workers who violate safety rules. This preventive system strengthens the accountability of workers who violate safety rules while imposing enhanced safety obligations on employers, aiming to establish a safety system where both workers and employers share responsibility. To this end, pilot tests will be conducted in areas with a high incidence of fatal accidents to establish a foundation for workers to recognize safety as a norm and actively participate. Therefore, based on analysis of international legislation and domestic case law, this paper highlights the need to revise the Serious Accident Punishment Act from a simple punishment-focused system to a prevention-focused and participatory one. It also proposes specific measures to improve worker awareness and institutional improvements, applying the regulatory structures of the Road Traffic Act, the Machinery and Equipment Act, and the Special Act on the Safety and Maintenance of Facilities. This study seeks to complement the limitations of the current criminal punishment-centered approach with a mobile safety system, thereby ensuring safe on-site work activities and achieving the legislative goal of preventing serious accidents.
